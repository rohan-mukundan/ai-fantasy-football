Why 1 RB backup now: In a 6-team league, 21 RBs sit on waivers — if your RB1 gets hurt, a replacement is waiting. In an 8-team league, only 16 RBs remain. When an elite RB goes down, every manager scrambles simultaneously. The waiver wire empties within 1 week. Your handcuff locks in the replacement value before the injury happens. Only handcuff true workhorse RBs with clear backup succession — committee backs' handcuffs inherit only 8–10 carries/game, not worth a roster spot.
8-Team Rules — From the Data
⚖️
BPA with an RB lean. Pure BPA still wins, but when two players are within 5 spots of each other in your value rankings and one is RB, take the RB. The 56% scarcity means they'll be harder to find late.
🔑
Draft your handcuff RB — once. PFR 2020–24: In 4 of 5 years, a top-3 RB missed 3+ games. Their handcuff averaged the RB8–15 range over those weeks. One handcuff on your bench earns its roster spot; two handcuffs wastes a FLEX spot.
⏰
QB in rounds 5–7, not earlier. 8 QBs get drafted in a full 8-team league. You need to be one of them — but the data shows the 6th-7th QB drafted still finishes QB4–8 most years. No need to reach in rounds 1–4.
🎯
TE: elite or stream. 6 TEs remain on waivers in an 8-team league — enough to stream but thin. The right answer depends on who's available: if a top-3 TE slips to round 3, take them. Otherwise, stream from the 6-player waiver pool and never waste a mid-round pick on a TE2.
📈
WR value is abundant — don't panic draft. 40 WRs on waivers at all times. PFR data shows 1 waiver WR finishes top-30 every year. You don't need WR depth in rounds 1–6 — fill your starting WR slots, then let the wire do the rest.
🚀
Rounds 9–12 are for upside, not safety. With a large waiver wire, safe late-round picks are redundant — you can replace them for free. Use rounds 9–12 for high-ceiling dart throws. One breakout from this range wins you weeks.
