RB handcuff is mandatory at 10 teams. PFR 2020–24: 3–4 top-20 ADP RBs miss 4+ games every year. In a 10-team league, 11 RBs remain on waivers after draft day. When an elite RB gets hurt, every manager adds his backup within 24 hours — and it's typically the #1 waiver priority. If you drafted him already, you own the value. If you didn't, you lose your starting spot for multiple weeks. WR backup is optional — 35 WRs on waivers means you can replace a hurt WR2 adequately without using a bench spot on one pre-emptively.
10-Team Rules — From the Data
🏃
RB-heavy draft is justified by the math. 69% of startable RBs get drafted. 3 RBs bust at top ADP every year. 11 RBs on waivers after drafts. This combination means: draft 3 real RBs in rounds 1–7, hold a handcuff, or face a season-ending hole when injuries hit.
🎯
TE: either elite or pure stream. Don't draft TE in rounds 4–8 if no elite is available. PFR data: TE2-tier players (rank 3–8 at TE) rarely justify a mid-round pick cost. Either grab the top-3 TE when he falls, or stream the 4 thin waiver options weekly.
⏰
QB by round 7 — no later. 10 QBs get drafted. In a 10-team league you need yours before the field locks in. The good news: per PFR data, QB7–10 still finishes QB4–8 most seasons. Draft late, not early.
🔑
Handcuff your RB1 in round 7 — not later. At 10 teams, other managers know the wire is thin. Smart managers target backups behind elite RBs immediately. If you wait until round 9–10, your handcuff is gone.
📊
WR value remains — don't overpay. PFR data: avg 3.5 WRs finish top-12 overall per year, and 1 undrafted WR cracks top-30. With 35 WRs on waivers, panic-drafting WR depth in rounds 5–8 is wasteful. Fill your 2 WR starters, add 1 WR FLEX candidate, stream the rest.
💡
This is the standard league — these rules are what conventional wisdom gets wrong. Most managers in 10-team leagues overdraft QB (top-5) and underdraft RB depth. The data says: QB in round 5–7, RB in rounds 1–4 and 7, and let the waiver wire handle WR/TE emergencies.
